Privacy Policy

Shotday keeps a log of your medication, weight and side effects. That is health information, so this policy explains exactly what the app stores, where it stays, and the few things that ever leave your phone.

The short version

  • No account, no Shotday server. There is nothing to sign up for and nowhere for us to store your log.
  • Your health log stays on your phone. Doses, weight, side effects and notes are never sent to us, to analytics, or to advertisers.
  • The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. Ads never receive your health data. Shotday Plus removes ads, and a Plus member's phone doesn't request them.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports are optional. You choose during setup, and can change it any time in Settings → Privacy. They never contain health data.
  • Purchases go through Google Play. We never see your name, email or payment details.
  • We never sell your data.

1. Who we are

Shotday (com.pkay.shotday, "the app") is developed and published by Levon Momchyan, an independent developer based in Armenia ("we", "us"). For the limited data described in sections 4 to 6, we are the data controller. You can reach us at levonmyan22@gmail.com.

2. What stays on your phone

Everything you enter is stored in the app's private storage on your device. It is not transmitted to us or to any third party by the app:

  • Medication plans: medication name, dose, schedule, reminder time, and an optional custom half-life.
  • Doses: date and time, dose, injection site, the pen or vial used, and notes.
  • Weight: weigh-ins and your goal weight.
  • Check-ins: side effects and their severity, food noise, and notes.
  • Supply: pens and vials, doses left, and expiry dates.
  • Settings: units, theme, language, reminders, and your privacy choices.
  • Technical state: whether Plus is active, when the last ad was shown (so ad limits work), and similar counters.

The estimated medication level, weight trend, insights and the doctor report are calculated on your phone from this data.

3. What we never collect

  • Your health log or any value from it, such as medication, dose, weight or side effects.
  • Your name, email address, phone number or any account details.
  • Your precise location. The app has no location permission.
  • Your contacts, photos or files, apart from a file you explicitly choose to import.

The app refuses, in code, to attach anything that looks like health data to analytics events.

4. Usage statistics and crash reports

We use Google Firebase Analytics to understand which features are used, and Firebase Crashlytics to find and fix crashes. Both are optional and controlled by two switches, shown during setup and in Settings → Privacy. They are off until you finish setup. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland they are off unless you turn them on; elsewhere they start switched on, and you can turn them off at any time.

What is sent

  • Usage events without values, such as "dose logged", "chart viewed" or "paywall opened", and which screen was open.
  • A random app-instance identifier, app version, device model, Android version, language, and country (derived by Google from the IP address).
  • For crashes: the technical error, the state of the app and the device at the time.

What is never sent

Medication names, doses, weights, side effects, notes or any other health information. Analytics does not collect the advertising ID: we switched that off, and we tell Firebase that analytics data may not be used for advertising.

How long it is kept

Analytics data is kept for no longer than 14 months. Crashlytics keeps crash reports for 90 days. Both are processed by Google as our service provider; see Firebase privacy and security.

5. Advertising

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob: small banners on some screens, occasional full-screen ads at natural breaks (never while you log), and rewarded ads only when you choose to watch one. Ads are limited to content rated PG or lower.

The Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (which gives an approximate location), device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Google uses this to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

  • Your health data is never shared with AdMob and never used to choose ads.
  • Consent: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks before any ad is personalised. In US states with privacy laws you can opt out of personalised ads. Change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy → Ad privacy choices.
  • Advertising ID: you can delete or reset it in Android settings under Privacy → Ads.
  • Shotday Plus: once Google Play confirms Plus, the app stops loading ads.

6. Purchases

Shotday Plus is sold through Google Play Billing. Google processes the payment under the Google Privacy Policy. The app receives only a purchase confirmation from Google Play (which product, its status, and a purchase token), checks its signature on your phone, and remembers whether Plus is active. We don't receive your name, email address or payment details.

7. App configuration and reviews

The app downloads settings from Firebase Remote Config, for example how often ads may appear or the wording of the Plus screen, so we can adjust them without an update. The request includes a random installation identifier, the app version and basic device information such as language. It contains no health data and no advertising ID.

After you have used the app for a while, it may ask Google Play to show its standard rating dialog (Google Play In-App Review). Your rating goes to Google Play, not to us.

9. Notifications and the widget

Reminders are scheduled on your phone. A dose reminder shows the medication name and dose, and the optional home-screen widget shows your next dose. Anyone who can see your screen may see them. To keep them private, hide sensitive notification content on the lock screen in Android settings, or don't add the widget.

10. Android backup

If Android backup is on, your Shotday log and settings are included in your phone's backup to your Google account, so they can be restored on a new phone. On Android 9 and newer, Shotday includes your log only when the backup is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock. On Android 12 and newer, the log can also move during a direct phone-to-phone transfer. These backups are stored by Google, not us. You can turn backup off in Android settings under System → Backup.

11. Export and sharing

  • Backup and CSV export: creates a file with your log in a place you choose. The file isn't encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
  • Import: reads only the backup file you select.
  • Doctor report: a PDF made on your phone. It leaves the phone only when you share it through Android's share menu, to the app or person you choose.

Once you share a file, the receiving app or person handles it under their own terms.

12. Retention and deletion

  • On your phone: your log stays until you delete entries, use Settings → Delete all data (which also switches off statistics and crash reports), or uninstall the app.
  • Statistics and crash reports: kept for the periods in section 4.
  • Advertising data: kept by Google under its own policies.

Because Shotday has no accounts, we can't link analytics or crash data to you by name. If you want it deleted, email us and we'll explain how to find your app's identifier so we can process the request.

13. Security

Your log is kept in the app's private storage, which Android isolates from other apps and encrypts on phones with a screen lock. Data sent to Google services uses encrypted connections (TLS). Purchase confirmations are checked against Google Play's signature. Please use a screen lock: anyone who can unlock your phone can open the app.

14. Legal bases and international transfers

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, we rely on:

  • Consent for usage statistics, crash reports and personalised ads. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings.
  • Legitimate interests for non-personalised ads that fund the free version, app configuration, and fraud prevention.
  • Contract for providing Shotday Plus after a purchase.

We don't process your health information on any server, so no legal basis for processing health data off your device is needed. Google may process the data in sections 4 to 7 in the United States and other countries, using safeguards such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and Standard Contractual Clauses.

15. Your rights

Depending on where you live, including under the GDPR, UK GDPR, and US state laws such as California's CCPA/CPRA, you may have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to the use of your personal data, to receive a copy of it, and to withdraw consent. For the data on your phone, you can exercise most of these yourself: view, edit, export or delete it in the app.

  • Sale and "sharing": we don't sell personal information. Where personalised ads count as "sharing" under US state law, you can opt out in Settings → Privacy → Ad privacy choices, or by deleting your advertising ID.
  • Consumer health data: under laws such as Washington's My Health My Data Act, we don't collect, share or sell consumer health data. Your health log never leaves your phone through us.
  • Complaints: you can contact us, and you have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

To make a request, email levonmyan22@gmail.com. We reply within 30 days.

16. Children

Shotday is meant for adults managing their own treatment and is not directed at anyone under 18. We don't knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has used the app, contact us and we'll help.

17. Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles data, we'll update this page and the "Last updated" date. For significant changes, we'll also tell you in the app before they take effect.
